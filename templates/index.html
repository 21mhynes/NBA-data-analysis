Overall conclusion

Bonus statistics connect to winning, but context matters

Reaching the bonus first is associated with a small advantage. Teams with more bonus attempts show a large raw winning advantage, while the late-game context changes how that comparison should be read. Bonus accuracy also relates to winning, but less strongly than attempts. Across the seasons studied, teams took fewer bonus free throws and made them more accurately.

The analysis identifies relationships; it does not establish that bonus timing or free throws cause a team to win.

What this analysis examines

Questions this project explores

The analysis looks at how bonus timing, free throws, game situations, teams, and seasons relate to winning.

01 · BONUS TIMING

Do teams that reach the bonus first in more periods than their opponents win more often?

02 · BY PERIOD

How does the first-to-bonus win rate vary by quarter and overtime?

03 · FOURTH QUARTER

How does win rate differ when a team first reaches the fourth-quarter bonus before versus during the final two minutes?

04 · BONUS ATTEMPTS

How does having more bonus free-throw attempts relate to winning, and what changes when late-game attempts are excluded?

05 · FREE-THROW ACCURACY

Is winning more closely associated with higher bonus-attempt volume or higher bonus free-throw accuracy?

06 · HOME COURT

How do home-court advantage and first-to-bonus results overlap?

07 · LEAGUE TRENDS

Across seasons, how have bonus attempts and free-throw accuracy changed?

08 · TEAM TRENDS

How have each team’s bonus attempts and accuracy changed from its first five to its most recent five seasons?

09 · TEAM CHANGES & WINNING

Are team changes in bonus attempts or accuracy associated with changes in winning percentage?
