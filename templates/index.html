NBA Bonus and Free-Throw Analysis

Research question
How does reaching the bonus first—and then shooting more free throws while in the bonus—affect an NBA team’s chance of winning?

This project also examines whether the relationship changes by quarter, home-court status, free-throw accuracy, team, and season. The figures below are loaded from analysis results stored in TiDB.

Overall conclusion

Bonus statistics connect to winning, but context matters

Reaching the bonus first is associated with a small advantage, but does not determine a game. Teams with more bonus attempts show a large raw winning advantage, though much of that gap disappears after late-game free throws are removed. Bonus accuracy also relates to winning, but less strongly than attempts. Across 30 seasons, teams have taken fewer bonus free throws and made them more accurately.

The strongest overall conclusion is that bonus statistics are connected to winning, but game situation and late-game strategy must be considered before interpreting the relationship as causal.

Limitations

What this analysis cannot establish

  • The analysis identifies relationships; it does not prove that reaching the bonus or receiving more attempts causes a team to win.
  • Intentional late-game fouling can give leading teams additional free throws.
  • Home teams already win more often, so home-court advantage is difficult to isolate from the bonus.
  • Overtime findings use a much smaller sample than regulation-quarter findings.
  • Team comparisons span different players, coaches, strategies, rule changes, and league environments.
  • Comparing the first five seasons with the last five summarizes long-term change, not every season-to-season variation.
